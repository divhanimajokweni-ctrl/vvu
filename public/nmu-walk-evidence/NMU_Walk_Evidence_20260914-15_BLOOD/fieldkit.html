PART 1 — The Leave-Behind

Show this. Don't explain it. Let them read it.

ProofBridge is a provenance-and-audit layer.

It records what happened in a system — a payment, a reading, a decision — as a cryptographic chain that any third party can verify without trusting the operator.

It does not detect lies. It makes it impossible to change the record after the fact. The bottleneck it removes is: "we can't prove what happened cheaply, portably, and across institutions that don't trust each other."

PART 2 — The Four Trust Bottlenecks

BottleneckPlain meaningDoes ProofBridge help?
ProvenanceWhere did this come from, and has it changed?Yes — core strength.
Evidence captureWas the ground truth recorded honestly?Partially — catches tampering after the fact, can't stop a lying sensor
Institutional trustDo we trust who runs the system?No — governance problem, not math
Cost of verificationThe check costs more than trustingYes — one hash verify, microseconds

PART 3 — The Three Questions

Q1. "In your field, what's the single biggest thing people cannot verify that they wish they could?"
Q2. "When something goes wrong — a bad decision, a falsified record, a failure — how is it caught today, and who pays for that check?"
Q3. "If you had a system that made every step of that record tamper-evident and cheap to check — what would it have to prove to you, personally, before you'd trust it?"
That third question is the whole trip. Write down every word.

On the horse itself

You're not carrying the academic record, or the krypte rebuild, or the codebase, or the Mk 0. You're carrying one paragraph (the leave-behind), one map (the taxonomy), and three questions.

Everything else — the fraud harness, the substrate, the audit chain, the dashboard, the R490 node — is what you say if asked. It's not what you lead with. Leading with it would make you a vendor. Leading with the questions makes you a researcher.

The single most valuable thing you can come back with is a stack of filled-in Q3 answers — the trust conditions lecturers name.

One thing to do before you walk: Photograph the faculty directory board. When you get back, you'll know which faculties you hit and which you missed.
